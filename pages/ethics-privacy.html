Data ethics, privacy and POPIA

The Protection of Personal Information Act applies to a hardware shop in Soshanguve exactly as it applies to a bank in Sandton. The difference is that the bank has a compliance department and the shop has Lerato.

Write the opening on why POPIA applies here (250–350 words)

Cover, in your own words:

  • Define personal information and processing in plain language. Make the point that "processing" includes simply storing a name in a book — the shop is already doing it.
  • Name the roles without jargon: the shop is the responsible party, the customer is the data subject, the Information Regulator is the referee.
  • Confront the obvious objection head-on: "we are too small for this". The Act has no small-business exemption. Explain what that does and does not mean in practice for a shop of this size.
  • Separate the two motivations honestly — the legal one (you must) and the ethical one (these are your neighbours, and this is a community where reputation travels faster than the Regulator does).
  • Verify and cite the commencement date of the operative provisions and the Regulator's role. Do not state them from memory.

Where to look it up: The Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 itself; the Information Regulator of South Africa's website and guidance notes.

The eight conditions, and what each one means at the counter

POPIA sets out eight conditions for lawful processing. They sound abstract until they are written out against a shop's actual day.

Section numbers must be checked against the Act before this is relied on.
#ConditionWhat it meansAt Lerato's
1Accountability
s.8		 Somebody is answerable for all of this. In a small shop that is the owner.Lerato is the Information Officer by default. She cannot delegate the responsibility, only the work.
2Processing limitation
ss.9–12		 Collect only what you need, lawfully, and with the person's knowledge.The shop does not need a customer's ID number to sell them a tap washer. Not collecting it is the cheapest compliance there is.
3Purpose specification
ss.13–14		 Know why you are holding it, say so, and do not keep it longer than you need it.The capture form will not save a customer without a recorded purpose and a retention date.
4Further processing limitation
s.15		 Do not quietly reuse information for something the person did not expect.Phone numbers given so a delivery could be arranged are not a marketing list.
5Information quality
s.16		 Keep it accurate and up to date.This is the same duty as the information-quality work on the data page — here it is also a legal one.
6Openness
ss.17–18		 Tell people what you are doing with their information.A short notice at the counter, in plain language, is worth more than a policy nobody reads.
7Security safeguards
ss.19–22		 Protect it, and report it if it is lost or breached.The laptop with the customer list needs a password and a backup. The s.22 duty to notify applies to a shop exactly as it does to a bank.
8Data subject participation
ss.23–25		 People may see what you hold about them, and have it corrected or deleted.This is the one spreadsheets genuinely cannot do. The demonstration answers it in one click.

Cover, in your own words:

  • Take each question and describe what answering it looks like on a Tuesday afternoon with a customer at the counter.
  • Question 1 is the strongest demonstration in the whole project — make sure the pitch lands it. A spreadsheet shop genuinely cannot produce a complete answer, and that is not a technicality, it is a failure to meet a right the customer actually has.

  • On retention: explain the direct conflict with SARS record-keeping requirements and how a shop resolves it — keep the transaction record for tax, strip or delete the personal detail that is no longer needed.

The honest point about the WhatsApp group

Customer details discussed in a staff WhatsApp group are personal information being processed on devices the shop does not control, with no retention, no access control and no way to answer a section 23 request. It is the most common and least noticed POPIA problem in South African small business, and no software fixes it. It is fixed by telling staff to stop.

Compliance is the floor, not the ceiling

Team writes this author: ____

Write the data ethics section (350–450 words — this is a marked sub-topic)

Cover, in your own words:

  • Separate legal from ethical clearly. Plenty of things are lawful and still wrong, and a shop lives on its reputation in a way a large company does not.
  • Work through at least two concrete dilemmas specific to this shop:
  • — The purchase history shows a customer buys building materials in a pattern suggesting they are struggling to finish a house. Is it ethical to use that to push credit at them?
  • — Staff can see every customer's purchase history. Should the counter assistant be able to see what a neighbour bought, and what they still owe?
  • — A supplier offers a discount in exchange for the shop's customer list. Lawful with consent, perhaps — but should it be done?
  • Cover transparency: people should not be surprised by what the shop knows about them.
  • Cover proportionality: collect what the job needs and nothing more. Link back to processing limitation.
  • Cover power: the customer has no real choice about handing over a phone number if they want an account. Consent given under that imbalance deserves more care, not less.
  • Close on the practical version of the ethical test: would you be comfortable if the customer read this record over your shoulder?

What the shop has to do, in order

  1. Appoint and register the Information Officer

    In a shop this size it is the owner, automatically. Registration with the Information Regulator is a separate step. Verify the current registration process and whether it carries a fee, and cite the source.

  2. Write down what you hold, and why

    One page. Every category of personal information, the reason for it, and how long it is kept. This is the document the Regulator asks for first.

  3. Put a short notice at the counter

    Plain language: what you collect, why, who you share it with, and how to ask for it or have it removed. Six sentences beats six pages.

  4. Stop collecting what you do not need

    The fastest compliance win available, and it is free. Every field you do not collect is a field you cannot lose, misuse or be asked about.

  5. Fix the consent register

    Anyone you cannot prove consented should be asked again or taken off the list.

  6. Secure the device, then test the backup

    Password, screen lock, and a restore that somebody has actually performed. A backup that has never been restored is a hope.

  7. Tell the staff the WhatsApp rule

    Costs nothing, closes the largest hole, and needs saying more than once.

On penalties

POPIA carries administrative fines and, for the most serious offences, criminal penalties. The team must verify the current maximum amounts and the circumstances in which they apply directly from the Act and the Regulator, and cite the source. Do not quote a figure you have seen repeated on a blog — it is the kind of claim a client will check.
