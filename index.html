Your shop already produces the data. It just isn't keeping it.

The problem, stated plainly

A spreadsheet stores a value. It does not store what that value means, where it came from, when it was last checked, whether anybody verified it, or what else depends on it. So every time the shop needs a quote, an invoice, a stock take or a supplier order, somebody retypes the same facts — and every retype is a fresh chance to be wrong.

The idea in one picture

Everything in this resource is a variation on one diagram. A fact enters the business once, is checked while the person who knows it is still standing there, and is then stored in a form that lets it be used again without being typed again.

The one idea to take away

The win is not that the computer is faster at typing. The win is that a fact captured properly once can be reused — on an invoice, in a reorder calculation, in a POPIA request, in the margin report — and all of those stay in agreement with each other because they are all looking at the same record.

Write the honest trade-offs section (about 200–300 words)

Cover, in your own words:

  • This is the section that earns the "grounded in real trade-offs" marks. Do not skip it and do not soften it.
  • Name what this costs: money every month, time to learn, the fortnight where things are slower, not faster.
  • Name what can go wrong: staff working around the system, a migration that imports the old mess, picking a vendor you cannot leave.
  • Name what should NOT be digitised yet, and why. Be specific about this shop.
  • Say plainly that a system does not fix a business that is not making money — it makes the numbers visible so the owner can act.

Evidence you can point at: The roadmap page carries the staged costs; keep this section about the honest downside and link there for the numbers.

How to use this resource

  1. Read the data page first

    Everything else assumes the capture-once idea. It is the longest page and the one worth your time.

  2. Open the demonstration beside it

    Each page links to the screen that shows the thing being described. Reading about a reorder calculation is not the same as looking at one for your own stock.

  3. Argue with the roadmap

    The staging is a proposal, not a prescription. The order should change to fit what is hurting the shop most right now.
